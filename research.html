Sequential Senescence-Targeted Maintenance for DFMO-Sensitized HRNB

High-Risk Neuroblastoma • Senescence & Senolytics

Purpose: Determine how difluoromethylornithine (DFMO)–driven senescence can be converted into rapid tumor clearance by adding a selective senolytic agent such as venetoclax during maintenance therapy. We quantify the temporal window in which polyamine depletion stabilizes growth arrest yet heightens BCL-2 dependence.

Map transcriptional and metabolic signatures that forecast response across cell lines, patient-derived organoids, and syngeneic mouse models. Sequential DFMO→senolytic schedules are benchmarked for apoptotic conversion, SASP modulation, and resistance liabilities such as MCL-1 induction.

Translate the most effective schedule into a clinical-trial concept by identifying pharmacodynamic biomarkers (polyamines, SASP cytokines, BH3 profiles) that can be tracked non-invasively in DFMO-maintained patients.

Patient impact: A well-timed senescence-to-apoptosis relay could significantly reduce minimal residual disease, lower relapse rates, and maintain the low-toxicity profile that families expect from DFMO maintenance.

Keywords: High-risk neuroblastoma, DFMO, venetoclax, senescence, senolytic therapy, BCL-2 dependence, polyamine metabolism

Adaptive-Immune Priming Strategy Layering Lipid Modulators onto OXA/DOX

Relapsed Neuroblastoma • Immunogenic Cell Death

Purpose: Evaluate whether adding the sphingosine kinase 2 inhibitor opaganib to oxaliplatin/doxorubicin (OXA/DOX) intensifies immunogenic cell death (ICD) in high-risk neuroblastoma. We monitor ER stress signals, calreticulin exposure, and HMGB1 release across MYCN-amplified and non-amplified models.

Determine how sphingolipid metabolite shifts influence antigen presentation, dendritic-cell priming, and adaptive immune memory in immune-competent mouse models, while maintaining dosing parameters that are clinically relevant.

Patient impact: A chemo-immunologic backbone that wakes up the immune system could extend remissions for relapsed patients without dramatically increasing toxicity.

Keywords: Relapsed neuroblastoma, opaganib, oxaliplatin, doxorubicin, sphingolipid signaling, immunogenic cell death, adaptive immunity

Patient-Derived mRNA Vaccine Therapy Targeting Multi-Antigen Neuroblastoma

Neuroblastoma • Custom Immunotherapy

Purpose: Translate sequencing of patient-derived neuroblastoma (NB) tumors and xenografts into multi-antigen mRNA vaccines that address MYCN-amplified disease and antigen heterogeneity. The NTxScribe platform enables rapid IVT production with GMP-minded quality control.

Benchmark vaccine candidates in murine models engrafted with patient-derived NB cells to measure TAA coverage, T-cell persistence, trafficking, and cytokine profiles relative to CAR T-cell benchmarks.

Develop an informatics pipeline that selects antigen combinations most likely to overcome immune exhaustion and suppressive tumor microenvironments, enabling personalized vaccine recipes within days of biopsy.

Patient impact: Families gain truly personalized immunotherapy options that may complement or replace antibody-based maintenance when anti-GD2 regimens plateau.

Keywords: Neuroblastoma, mRNA vaccine, tumor-associated antigens, MYCN amplification, patient-derived xenograft, immunotherapy

Computational Neoantigen Discovery for Individualized mRNA Vaccines

Precision Immunotherapy • Neoantigen & Neoepitope Discovery

Purpose: Use computational genomics, epitope prediction, and our extensive patient database to identify novel neoantigens and neoepitopes in a patient-specific manner. We integrate tumor sequencing, expression data, and clinical annotation to prioritize targets that are unique to each child’s cancer.

Build individualized mRNA vaccine designs that encode the highest confidence neoantigen set for each patient, aiming to focus immune recognition on tumor-specific mutations while avoiding normal tissue targets and reducing off-target effects.

Continuously refine the discovery pipeline by learning from our growing database of patient samples, treatment histories, and tumor biology so vaccine selection becomes faster, more precise, and more clinically actionable over time.

Patient impact: This approach supports truly personalized cancer vaccines tailored to the molecular fingerprint of each patient’s tumor, with the goal of improving antitumor immunity while minimizing unintended toxicity.

Keywords: Neoantigens, neoepitopes, personalized mRNA vaccines, computational genomics, precision immunotherapy, patient-specific targeting, pediatric oncology

Combinatorial Targeted Therapy in DIPG

Precision Oncology • Combinatorial Targeted Therapy in DIPG

Purpose: Evaluate whether rational combinations of temozolomide, trametinib, Ribociclib, and Everolimus improve the response of diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG), a pediatric brain tumor with limited therapeutic options. Patient-derived DIPG cell lines with and without the H3K27M mutation (SL01469, SL00731, SU-DIPG-33) were exposed to single agents and high/low-dose combinations for 72 hours with viability and protein endpoints.

Combination regimens triggered roughly 45–50% cell death across several models while reducing phospho-p70S6K, phospho-Rb, and phospho-ERK1/2 signaling alongside lower MGMT expression, indicating reinforced cell-cycle disruption and DNA-damage responses relative to monotherapies.

Patient impact: Supports a combination targeted therapy strategy for DIPG and presents a preclinical rationale to upgrade regimens where current standards remain ineffective.

Keywords: DIPG, temozolomide, trametinib, Ribociclib, Everolimus, H3K27M, combination targeted therapy

Patient impact: Ensuring heart health protects access to effective polyamine-targeted maintenance without unexpected toxicity.

Keywords: Polyamine inhibition, DFMO, AMXT-1501, cardiotoxicity, safety modeling, pediatric oncology

Patient impact: Offers a targeted-therapy roadmap where options are otherwise limited to intensive surgery, chemotherapy, and high-risk radiation.

LIN28/let-7 Axis Modulation in Infant ETMR via Polyamine Restriction

Embryonal Tumors • Differentiation Therapy

Explore complementary agents that stabilize the LIN28/let-7 axis without imposing neurotoxic radiation burdens on very young patients.

Patient impact: Offers a targeted, potentially low-toxicity option for infants with ETMR who cannot tolerate conventional radiation.
