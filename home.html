Our Research Meet the Team

The Sholler Lab is focused on translational research in pediatric oncology acting as the preclinical research team of the Beat Childhood Cancer Research Consortium (BCCRC).

Mission Statement

Our mission is to cure childhood cancer by harnessing precision medicine to better understand and target each child’s unique tumor. We are dedicated to uncovering genomic and biological drivers of pediatric solid and brain tumors and disrupting these pathways through targeted drugs and immunotherapies. By bridging laboratory discovery with innovative clinical trials, we aim to deliver safer, more effective treatments that improve outcomes for children.

Research Objectives

Precision Medicine Program

Personalized medicine through the Precision Medicine Program is a major focus of the lab. We integrate genomic sequencing, bioinformatic analysis, and preclinical validation to identify and prioritize therapeutic targets and combinations for pediatric solid tumors.

Collaboration Model

We work with Beat Childhood Cancer collaborators internationally through shared samples, genomic sequencing data, and bioinformatic support. Multi-site consortia means that promising ideas in the lab can move into trials across dozens of hospitals, giving more children access to new therapies.

Our goal is simple: move the best science to the bedside as quickly and safely as possible.

Neuroblastoma

Neuroblastoma is a cancer that develops from immature nerve cells arising from the sympathetic nervous system and affecting children <6 yrs of age presenting as a tumor in the abdomen, chest, or spine.

The lab focuses on novel therapies for neuroblastoma with an emphasis on DFMO-based combinations, ALK-targeted therapies, and immune-modulating strategies to reduce relapse and improve survival in high-risk disease.

Sarcomas

Ewing Sarcoma and Osteosarcoma are rare bone and soft-tissue cancers in adolescents. Rhabdomyosarcoma affects soft tissue, connective tissue, and bone.

Through the BCCRC, we advance genomic discovery and precision medicine to improve frontline and relapse therapies. We optimize inhibitor–based combinations and support pilot studies to deliver more effective, less toxic treatment options for children with recurrent or refractory disease.

CNS Tumors

We study rare and aggressive pediatric brain tumors including diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG), atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor (AT/RT), embryonal tumor with multilayered rosettes (ETMR), and medulloblastoma.

Current preclinical work evaluates inhibitors and combination strategies (e.g., DFMO, GSK3B, HDAC inhibition), with the goal of advancing promising approaches into clinical trials.

How Our Research Reaches Patients

Our projects are built around direct patient impact: lowering relapse, expanding options at recurrence, and creating new ways to match therapies to each child’s tumor.

Lowering Relapse Risk in High-Risk Neuroblastoma

DFMO improves survival but induces senescence rather than direct cell death. By adding the senolytic agent venetoclax after DFMO, we aim to clear residual senescent tumor cells and decrease the chance that children on DFMO maintenance will relapse.

Expanding Options for Relapsed Neuroblastoma

We are testing the SphK2 inhibitor opaganib with oxaliplatin and doxorubicin to boost immunogenic cell death and strengthen the immune response. These data are intended to support early-phase trials for children with relapsed or refractory high-risk neuroblastoma.

Custom mRNA Immunotherapy Vaccines for Pediatric Solid Tumors

We generate patient-specific mRNA immunotherapy vaccines based on each child's tumor genetics using the NTxScribe system. These vaccines harness the immune system to target tumor-specific mutations and enhance standard therapies.

New Strategies for Rare Pediatric Brain Tumors

For ETMR and ATRT, we are evaluating GSK3B and HDAC inhibitor combinations in preclinical models to identify regimens that are strong enough, and safe enough, to move toward first-in-child targeted and combination trials.

BCC Student Research Scholars Program

Our laboratory also offers the BCC Student Research Scholars Program for motivated undergraduate students interested in gaining hands-on experience in translational pediatric oncology research. BCC Student Research Scholars work closely with scientists and trainees in the lab, contributing to active research projects while learning techniques in experimental design, data analysis, and scientific communication. Scholars also shadow in clinic alongside physicians, including Dr. Sholler, gaining firsthand exposure to how novel therapies and active clinical trials move research from bench to bedside.

This program is ideal for students considering future careers in medicine, biomedical research, or biotechnology and who want to experience how discoveries in the lab can move toward clinical trials that help children with cancer.

Interested in upcoming positions or rotation opportunities? Please contact the lab at BCCLab@pennstatehealth.psu.edu to start the conversation.

Future collaborators

Our Technical Expertise

Come to us for end-to-end mRNA production, translational model systems, and data-rich combination testing that accelerate pediatric precision oncology. All collaborations are welcome—let’s build what you need.

mRNA Vaccine Design & Production

Custom mRNA vaccine design with rapid IVT production, payload optimization, and immunogenicity assessment. We provide formulation support and biomarker discovery to guide personalized immunotherapy approaches for pediatric solid tumors.

Genomic Analysis & Biomarker Discovery

Tumor profiling and precision oncology workflows that identify actionable mutations, guide therapeutic targeting, and predict treatment response for pediatric solid tumors from long-read, single-cell, and targeted sequencing workflows.

Combination Testing & PD

High-throughput viability, imaging, and phospho-protein analyses to prioritize rational combinations and uncover biomarkers that guide the next protocol.

Patient-Derived Models

Organoids, xenografts, and DIPG/neuroblastoma cell lines preserved with microenvironment cues to preview toxicity, efficacy, and resistance mechanisms before the clinic.
